# Heatmaps of LD matrices comparing EAS and EUR ancestries at the FADS locus
**Date:** August 12, 2026

## Main Points
Figure 5 compares LD matrices of EAS (left) against EUR (right) at the FADS locus, encompassing FADS1 and FADS2. The heatmap is scaled conventionally, according to r2 values (0.0 < r2 < 1.0), thus showing squared correlation between SNPs. X-axis has ticks according to the index of the variant in list of shared-SNPs, discounting multi-allelic sites, because of uneven shared variant spacing and the plt.imshow's uniform grid - the heatmap format. EAS and EUR share 552 variants at identical locations extracted from a chosen 270kb window around the FADS region. 

Both EAS and EUR heatmaps show LD blocks towards the beginning of the aligned segment, roughly from 0-100 idx. Similarly, both ancestries have LD blocks on the FADS1 and FADS2 genes - notably, very strong LD signals (regional mean r2 in EAS = 0.814, mean r2 in EUR = 0.749) at FADS1, from 152-173 idx. From 414 idx to the end of the aligned segment, both ancestries show similar linkage: two LD blocks, one large followed by one small, with other smaller linkage signals that don't appear as blocks. 

The EAS ancestry shows, notably, more long-range linkage than EUR. In the 100-270kb bin, EAS has an mean r2 of 0.0271 while EUR only has 0.0121, a 2.25x disparity. EAS has 585 samples vs. the 633 samples of EUR. Sampling noise can be estimated using the $1/n$ bias: calculated as $ 1/(585-1) - 1/(633-1) = 0.00013 $. Checking for the role of noise in this shown disparity: observed disparity accounts for $0.0271 - 0.0121 = 0.0150$, $0.00013/0.0150 = 0.00867$ or 0.867% < 1.0% of the disparity - negligible. Thus, the long-range linkage in EAS is real, not an artifact of panel size. This shows also how a larger EAS panel - closer to the size of the EUR panel, can reduce sampling noise, linking to the IMPACT study which used 105 EAS samples compared against 659 EUR samples. 

EUR possesses cleaner LD blocking mostly centered along the diagonal. EAS, on the other hand, shows long-range linkage, e.g., of variants at 200 idx moderately correlating with variants in the range 414-500 idx; EUR is much weaker - barely visible. EUR has a clean LD block from 138-414 idx whereas, in that region, EAS seems to split into two blocks. EAS has a global mean r2 of 0.085079 whereas EUR has a mean r2 of 0.075545. EAS shows roughly 1.13x more linkage globally than EUR. The largest linkage contrast between EAS and EUR in the regions examined, however, is 5.7x, in the linking of 0-100 idx to 138-276 idx: EAS has mean LD of 0.081 while EUR has mean LD of 0.014.

The Pearson correlation of LD values, corr(r2), between populations, is 0.742407: the coefficient of determination is $ 0.742407^2 = 0.551168$ or roughly 55%. Thus, the LD values of the two populations are moderately correlated with each other. Out of the total 152,076 pairs computed, 207 pairs were strong in EAS but weak in EUR (r2>=0.8 is strong, r2<=0.2 is weak); 124 pairs were strong in EUR but weak in EAS; 2602 pairs strong in both. The former two values (the 331 discordant pairs) can be used for credible-set shrinkage while the 2602 pairs indicates a low-resolvability set of shared variants, pointing to a credible set ceiling persistent in multi-ancestry fine-mapping.

The FADS locus is a cluster of genes FADS1, FADS2, and FADS3; these encode desaturase enzymes that catalyze the rate-limiting step of long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid (LC-PUFAs) production (e.g., omega-3 and omega-6). The FADS region is known to be selected among human populations, due to dietary and agricultural traditions. The EAS population data taken from the 1000 Genomes Project pools genomes from Chinese, Vietnamese, and Japanese groups, while the EUR population data pools English, Italian, Spanish, and Finnish groups. Thus, linkage trends cannot be taken as evident in all EAS or EUR populations. EAS ancestry is known to exhibit the founder effect whereby a smaller founding population would lead to a genetic drift that isn't attributed to selection - this may explain the long-range LDs seen in EAS. Selection changes allele frequencies while genetic drift creates long-range LD. To test this out, we can isolate a subpopulation of EAS and conducting the same study again and check whether the long-range LDs survive.
